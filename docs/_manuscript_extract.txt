[7|Normal] Abstract
[8|Normal] Philippine government identification documents and certificates are issued by separate agencies. Each agency publishes its own requirements, fees, processing time, and office locations, commonly in a Citizen’s Charter required by Republic Act No. 11032. An applicant who needs a passport, a Philippine Identification System record, a clearance, or a professional license must still discover which documents are jointly required, which documents are alternatives, and which offices to visit and in what order. This study specifies that task as a data-mining problem on the Philippine Government Document Requirements and Acquisition Pathways dataset (PH-GovDocPath). Official .gov.ph pages and Citizen’s Charters supply the services, requirements, and offices. OpenStreetMap supplies a coordinate only when an office address can be located. Each service is one transaction. Each document that the charter requires together with the others is one item. Apriori, FP-Growth, and Eclat mine the same transaction matrix. Support, confidence, and lift evaluate the rules. A rule is drawn as a route only when its lift shows a positive association and the current charter of the consequent service still states the requirement. The work follows the six phases of the Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining (CRISP-DM). Collection from official pages is in progress. Association-rule metrics and mapped distances will be reported after the three algorithms are run. The study stores no applicant record and does not estimate an individual’s chance of approval.
[9|Normal] Keywords: Association Rule Mining, CRISP-DM, Geospatial Mapping, Government Documents, Philippines
[10|Normal] Introduction
[11|Normal] Obtaining a Philippine government identification document is a sequence of documents and office visits. A birth certificate, a national identification record, a clearance, a license, and a passport are issued by different authorities. The applicant has to learn the requirement list of each authority and the order in which those requirements can be satisfied. The list and the office are published, but they are published apart from one another, and each publication covers the service of one agency.
[12|Normal] Republic Act No. 11032, the Ease of Doing Business and Efficient Government Service Delivery Act of 2018, requires every covered agency and local government unit to post a current Citizen’s Charter. The charter must state the checklist of requirements, the procedure, the responsible officer, the maximum time, the fees, and the way to file a complaint (Republic Act No. 11032, 2018). Republic Act No. 11055 established the Philippine Identification System as a single national identification system and directed agencies to honor the PhilSys number, once authenticated, as proof of identity (Republic Act No. 11055, 2018). The Philippine Statistics Authority publishes the supporting documents accepted for PhilSys registration. Primary documents include a PSA-issued certificate of live birth together with one government identification document, or a passport issued by the Department of Foreign Affairs, a Unified Multi-purpose Identification card, or a driver’s license issued by the Land Transportation Office. Where none of those is available, secondary documents such as an NBI clearance may be presented if they meet the stated conditions (Philippine Statistics Authority, n.d.). The National Bureau of Investigation, for its part, states that an NBI clearance application requires two government-issued identification cards (National Bureau of Investigation, n.d.). These pages already show the dependency the study is concerned with: a document from one agency is an input to a service of another.
[13|Normal] The dataset defined for this study, PH-GovDocPath, covers that dependency for a stated set of services. The services are civil-registry documents of the Philippine Statistics Authority, the passport of the Department of Foreign Affairs, PhilSys registration, the NBI clearance, the Land Transportation Office driver’s license, the professional license of the Professional Regulation Commission, voter registration of the Commission on Elections, the business name of the Department of Trade and Industry, membership services of the Social Security System, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG, member services of the Government Service Insurance System, and local permits of the City Government of Davao. Davao City is the local-government case because the study is conducted at Mapúa Malayan Colleges Mindanao. National services are included wherever the responsible agency publishes them. Primary data are limited to official government websites on .gov.ph domains and to official Citizen’s Charters. OpenStreetMap is used for the coordinate of an office whose address appears in those sources. Kaggle, the UCI repository, Wikipedia, and commercial blogs are not primary sources.
[14|Normal] Work on public-service description, on frequent itemsets, and on spatial access each solves part of this problem. A Citizen’s Charter and a public-service model describe one service, its provider, and its inputs (Gerontas et al., 2021; Gerontas et al., 2022; San Jose, 2023). Frequent-itemset mining finds items that occur together across transactions (Agrawal et al., 1993; Luna et al., 2019). A geographic information system can measure the travel that separates a person from a service location (Luo & Wang, 2003; Haklay & Weber, 2008). What remains is a dataset in which Philippine services, jointly required documents, alternative documents, and office locations are stored together; a comparison of Apriori, FP-Growth, and Eclat on the transaction matrix built from that dataset; and a route that places the offices of a rule only after the rule has been checked against the charter.
[15|Normal] This paper specifies that study. The introduction states the problem, the questions, and the objectives. The review places the study against public-service description, association-rule mining, CRISP-DM, and geospatial access. The method follows the six CRISP-DM phases and states how alternatives are encoded, how the three algorithms are compared, and how a kept rule becomes an office route. Numerical rule results are reserved for the run of those algorithms.
[16|Normal] Statement of the Problem
[17|Normal] An applicant cannot read a single charter and recover the full acquisition path. The charter of the passport office does not contain the charter of the civil registrar, and the page that lists a required document does not, by itself, give the coordinate of the office that issues it. Requirements that the charter offers as alternatives are a further source of error. If a passport, a UMID card, and a driver’s license are each sufficient for PhilSys registration, placing all three in one basket would record a joint requirement that the charter does not state. Office locations are published separately from requirement lists, and a missing coordinate cannot be replaced by a guessed point.
[18|Normal] The study therefore addresses the absence of one official transaction dataset that links services, jointly required documents, alternative documents, and office locations, and the absence of a checked association-rule procedure that can turn a recurring document pattern into a sequence and a route. The procedure has to keep the wording of the charter, compare Apriori, FP-Growth, and Eclat on the same matrix, and withhold a route when the rule fails the charter check.
[19|Normal] Research Questions
[20|Normal] The study is guided by the following questions:
[21|Normal] 1. How can official service descriptions, jointly required documents, alternative documents, and office locations be represented as one transaction dataset?
[22|Normal] 2. Which document combinations recur across services when Apriori, FP-Growth, and Eclat are applied to that dataset at the same minimum support?
[23|Normal] 3. Which of those rules remain reliable when support, confidence, and lift are computed and the rule is checked against the current Citizen’s Charter?
[24|Normal] 4. How can a kept rule be turned into an ordered document sequence and a geospatial route among the offices that issue or accept those documents?
[25|Normal] Research Objectives
[26|Normal] The general objective is to perform association rule mining on PH-GovDocPath in order to discover frequent document-requirement patterns, to compare Apriori, FP-Growth, and Eclat on support, confidence, and lift, and to link the rules that pass the charter check to office locations for route planning. Specifically, the study aims:
[27|Normal] 1. To build and describe a unified dataset of government services, required documents, requirement conditions, and office locations, using only official pages, Citizen’s Charters, and OpenStreetMap coordinates that can be retrieved for published addresses.
[28|Normal] 2. To encode each service as one transaction and each jointly required document as an item, keeping alternative documents out of the primary basket, and to mine that matrix with Apriori, FP-Growth, and Eclat at the same minimum support.
[29|Normal] 3. To evaluate the rules with support, confidence, and lift, to compare the three algorithms on the itemsets they return and on the time they take, and to accept a rule for routing only when the current charter still states the requirement.
[30|Normal] 4. To recommend a document-acquisition sequence from each accepted rule and to map that sequence onto the issuing and receiving offices for which coordinates were retrieved.
[31|Normal] Significance of the Study
[32|Normal] The study organizes public requirement text so that a document dependency can be read across agencies and then placed on a map. The groups below are the ones that can use that organization.
[33|Normal] Applicants. A checked rule and its route state which documents are obtained before a target service and which offices issue them. The route is an aid to planning a visit. It is not a decision on the application.
[34|Normal] Frontline staff. Staff who explain requirements can point to the charter passage that supports a rule, and can see when a document is an alternative rather than a joint requirement. San Jose (2023) reported that service users in one Philippine district often transacted without knowing the Citizen’s Charter. A traceable requirement pattern is a way to make that charter usable across agencies.
[35|Normal] Agencies that publish charters. The dataset makes gaps visible: a service with no parsed requirements, an office with no retrieved coordinate, or a rule that the charter no longer supports. Those gaps are properties of the published text and of the collection, and they can be corrected at the source.
[36|Normal] Researchers. The transaction design, the comparison of the three frequent-itemset algorithms on a sparse requirement matrix, and the charter check before a route is drawn are reusable for other public-service catalogues. The study also shows where CRISP-DM’s deployment phase is carried out, which many CRISP-DM reports omit (Schröer et al., 2021).
[37|Normal] Review of Related Literature
[38|Normal] Citizen’s Charters and Public-Service Description
[39|Normal] The legal form of a Philippine service description is the Citizen’s Charter. Republic Act No. 11032 requires a comprehensive checklist of requirements, the procedure, the persons responsible, the maximum time, the documents to be presented, the fees, and the complaint procedure (Republic Act No. 11032, 2018). That list is exactly the material an association-rule study needs, provided the list is read as the agency wrote it. Joint requirements and alternatives are both present in these lists. PhilSys registration is the clearest national example: some documents are accepted in place of others, and a birth certificate may be paired with a separate identification document (Philippine Statistics Authority, n.d.).
[40|Normal] Publishing the charter does not, by itself, make the path known to the person who needs the service. San Jose (2023) evaluated Citizen’s Charter compliance among local government frontline offices in the 4th District of Camarines Sur. The offices posted charter information, yet service users were often unaware of it and completed transactions by following routine practice. The finding that matters here is narrow and empirical: the charter can exist while the applicant still lacks a usable account of what to bring and where to go next.
[41|Normal] Outside the Philippines, public-service research has tried to give catalogues a shared structure. Gerontas et al. (2021) reviewed public-service models and synthesized the elements that recur: the service, the provider, the input, the output, and the rule that governs access. The Core Public Service Vocabulary Application Profile is the European specification built for that purpose. It can record the public service, the responsible organization, the evidence a person must supply, the cost, and the area in which the service is available (Gerontas et al., 2022). Gerontas et al. (2022) published part of an official regional catalogue as linked data that conforms to this profile, and they documented the mismatch between a local citizen’s guide and the shared vocabulary. Their result is a process for describing services so that one catalogue can point at another.
[42|Normal] Those models establish the fields a service record should have. They do not mine which evidence documents co-occur across services, and they do not produce an ordered visit among offices. PH-GovDocPath uses the same kinds of fields — service, agency, requirement, condition, and location — and then subjects the joint requirements to association rule mining. The charter remains the authority for whether a mined pattern is still true.
[43|Normal] Association Rule Mining
[44|Normal] Association rule mining searches a set of transactions for items that occur together. Agrawal, Imieliński, and Swami (1993) defined the task on retail baskets: a rule X → Y means that transactions containing X tend also to contain Y. Support is the share of transactions that contain both sides. Confidence is the share of transactions containing X that also contain Y. The interestingness of a rule is not settled by those two numbers alone. Lift compares the observed co-occurrence with the co-occurrence expected if the two sides were independent. A lift above 1 is a positive association (Han et al., 2012). In this study the transaction is a government service and the item is a document the service requires jointly with the others. A rule is then a statement about services, not about people.
[45|Normal] Three algorithms dominate the search for frequent itemsets, and the study compares those three because they represent the main search strategies. Apriori generates candidate itemsets level by level and prunes a candidate whose subset is already infrequent (Agrawal & Srikant, 1994). The pruning is sound, and it is expensive when the candidates are many or the frequent itemsets are long. FP-Growth stores the transactions in a frequent-pattern tree and grows itemsets from that tree, so it does not materialize the candidate sets Apriori enumerates (Han et al., 2000). Eclat transposes the data. Each item keeps the list of transaction identifiers in which it appears, and itemsets are formed by intersecting those lists in a depth-first search (Zaki, 2000). A vertical layout is a natural fit for a sparse matrix, which a requirement matrix is expected to be: most services require a few documents, not the whole catalogue.
[46|Normal] Borgelt (2012) reviewed the data structures that make this search practical, including candidate pruning, the frequent-pattern tree, and vertical intersection. Luna, Fournier-Viger, and Ventura (2019) reviewed twenty-five years of frequent-itemset mining and separated exhaustive search from later approximate and parallel variants. For a catalogue of a few hundred services, an exhaustive search is appropriate. The patterns have to be complete enough to check against a charter, so a sample of itemsets would leave the charter check without a defined population of rules.
[47|Normal] Heaton (2016) compared Apriori, Eclat, and FP-Growth while holding the number of transactions fixed and varying frequent-itemset density and maximum transaction size. Eclat and FP-Growth remained faster than Apriori as density and transaction length grew. That comparison used generated baskets. PH-GovDocPath is one real matrix of published requirements. The study therefore does not treat Heaton’s ranking as a result to be copied. It uses the same idea for a fair comparison: one matrix, one minimum support, and three algorithms, with time and agreement reported on that matrix. Implementations differ in ways that can swamp the algorithm. The three miners will be taken from one library, SPMF, which provides Apriori, FP-Growth, and Eclat (Fournier-Viger et al., 2016).
[48|Normal] CRISP-DM as the Process Model
[49|Normal] The Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining was written so that a mining project would state its purpose, understand the data, prepare a matrix, build a model, evaluate the model against the purpose, and deploy the result (Wirth & Hipp, 2000; Chapman et al., 2000). The six phases are business understanding, data understanding, data preparation, modeling, evaluation, and deployment. The cycle returns to an earlier phase when the evaluation shows that the question or the matrix was wrong.
[50|Normal] Schröer, Kruse, and Gómez (2021) reviewed data-mining studies that claimed to follow CRISP-DM. The model was still the usual process. The phase most often missing was deployment: studies stopped once a model had been scored. For PH-GovDocPath the deployment phase is the route. A rule that is not joined to an office sequence has not answered the fourth research question. The return loop is also specific. A rule whose lift is high, but whose consequent the current charter does not require, is sent back to business understanding, where “requirement” is defined, and to data preparation, where joint items and alternatives are separated. Figure 1, in the method, draws that cycle.
[51|Normal] Geospatial Access to Service Locations
[52|Normal] A requirement pattern becomes a route only when each document is joined to a place. Luo and Wang (2003) synthesized two geographic measures of access to primary care and applied them in the Chicago region. Both measures depend on a travel threshold and on the supply of service inside that threshold. Their setting is health care, and this study does not estimate a shortage area. The transferable point is that access is travel between a person and a facility, and that a facility without a coordinate cannot enter the measure.
[53|Normal] OpenStreetMap is a public geographic database maintained by its contributors, and it has been described as a usable source of street geometry where official road data are awkward to obtain (Haklay & Weber, 2008). This study uses it in a limited way. An office address printed in a charter or on a .gov.ph page is geocoded against OpenStreetMap. If the address resolves, the latitude and longitude are stored with the source of the match. If it does not resolve, the coordinate is left empty. A travel leg is computed on the OpenStreetMap road network only between offices that both have coordinates. The leg is the connection between two stops of a document sequence. It is not a model of city-wide accessibility, and it is not a prediction of waiting time inside the office.

[59|Normal] Conceptual Framework
[60|Normal] The study uses an input–process–output frame whose process is CRISP-DM. The input is the official text of the selected services: the agency, the service name, the requirement and its condition, the fee and processing time when the page states them, the office address, and the OpenStreetMap coordinate when the address resolves. Pages that are not official, and coordinates that were not retrieved, are not inputs. The process builds five tables, forms one transaction per service from the jointly required documents, mines that matrix with Apriori, FP-Growth, and Eclat, and scores every rule with support, confidence, and lift. The output is the set of frequent document patterns, the comparison of the three algorithms, and a route for each rule that the charter check accepts. A rule that fails the charter check returns to the definition of a requirement and to the encoding of alternatives. Figure 1 shows that return. Figure 2 shows the system that carries a source page through to a route, and it shows the sources that are not connected.

[65|Normal] Research Design
[66|Normal] The design is a CRISP-DM study of one dataset. The mining task is association rule mining, not classification. There is no labeled outcome such as “application approved,” and no individual applicant appears in the matrix. The unit of analysis is the government service. The comparison among Apriori, FP-Growth, and Eclat is descriptive. It reports the itemsets each algorithm returns, the overlap of those itemsets, and the time each algorithm takes on this matrix. It does not test a hypothesis about a larger population of unseen services, because the matrix is the population of services defined for the study.

[73|Normal] Business Understanding
[74|Normal] Business understanding fixes the decision the mining result is allowed to support. The decision is which documents to obtain before a target service, and which offices to visit for those documents. The decision is not whether an application will be granted, how long the queue will be, or which agency should change its policy.
[75|Normal] A result is successful when four conditions hold. First, every item in the mined matrix is a document named in an official page or charter, and the source address is stored with the item. Second, Apriori, FP-Growth, and Eclat are run on that matrix at the same minimum support, and each rule is reported with support, confidence, and lift. Third, a rule is eligible for a route only when its lift is greater than 1 and a reader can point to the current charter sentence that still requires the consequent given the antecedent. Fourth, the route draws a travel leg only between offices whose coordinates were retrieved. A leg with an empty coordinate is marked unlocated. These conditions are the acceptance tests of the study. They will be applied after the run. They are not results of a run that has already been scored.
[76|Normal] The service boundary is the set named in the introduction. Adding an agency later is a new cycle of data understanding, not a silent extension of the rule list. The local-government case is Davao City. National services remain in the matrix wherever their charters are published, because a Davao applicant still obtains a passport, a PhilSys record, or an NBI clearance from the agency that issues it.
[77|Normal] Data Understanding
[78|Normal] Data understanding inventories the pages before they are reshaped. For each agency the collectors record the official domain, the charter or service page, and the date the page was retrieved. The attributes follow the project proposal. They are the agency, the service, the target document, the transaction type, the applicant type, the requirement name, the requirement type, the requirement condition, the issuing agency, the application method, whether an appointment is required, the processing time, the fee, the office location, the service category, the document format, the quantity, and the source address. An attribute that the page does not state is left empty. A fee, a processing time, or a coordinate is never filled by estimation.
[79|Normal] The project proposal recorded the collection as it stood then: 757 cleaned records, 727 records retrieved live from agency pages, and about 1,498 data objects across the tables. Those counts describe progress at the time of the proposal. The manuscript will replace them with the counts of the matrix that is mined, including the number of services, the number of distinct documents, the distribution of items per transaction, the number of offices, and the number of offices with and without a coordinate. Services whose requirement list could not be parsed are counted and excluded from the matrix, with the reason stored.
[80|Normal] Two quality checks belong to this phase. The first is provenance: every requirement row points at a .gov.ph address or at a charter file obtained from such an address. The second is the joint-versus-alternative reading. A page that says a birth certificate and a government identification document are both presented is a joint requirement. A page that says a passport or a UMID card or a driver’s license will be accepted is an alternative group. The two readings are stored in the requirement type. They are not collapsed during data understanding.
[81|Normal] Data Preparation
[82|Normal] Preparation turns the pages into the five tables in Figure 2 and then into the transaction matrix. Collection uses HTTP requests against official pages, an HTML parser for services, requirements, fees, procedures, and office text, and a headless browser only when a page does not return its content to a plain request. Office addresses are geocoded with OpenStreetMap. The collectors respect the site’s robots.txt and a request delay. They do not collect names, application numbers, or any other personal record. When a site blocks access, the gap is filled by a manual reading of the same official page, or it is left empty. It is not filled from a blog or from a secondary compilation.
[83|Normal] The five tables are agency, service, requirement, office, and transaction. Agency stores the name and the official domain. Service stores the service name, the agency, the applicant type, the fee and processing time when published, and the source address. Requirement stores the document name, the requirement type, and the condition text. Office stores the published address, the latitude and longitude when geocoding succeeds, and the geocode source. Transaction stores one row per service in the primary matrix.
[84|Normal] The primary matrix counts each service once. Its items are the documents the charter requires jointly. Alternative documents are stored on the requirement row and are not added to that basket. Putting every accepted PhilSys document into one basket would create the itemset {birth certificate, passport, UMID, driver’s license} and would treat substitutes as if they were demanded together. A second, sensitivity matrix expands each alternative group into its own transaction so that the effect of that choice can be seen. Rules from the sensitivity matrix are labeled as such and are not mixed into the primary rule list. Document names are normalized after a written synonym list, so that “PSA birth certificate” and “certificate of live birth issued by the PSA” are one item when the page shows they are the same document. The synonym list is part of the dataset release. A name that cannot be resolved stays in its page wording and is not merged by guesswork.
[85|Normal] Figure 3 shows the encoding with four services. The rows illustrate how a transaction is read. They are not rules discovered by the algorithms. The passport row follows the pattern used in the project proposal to explain the encoding. The transaction that is mined will follow the Department of Foreign Affairs page retrieved during collection, which may name further documents. The PhilSys row follows the published distinction between a paired birth certificate and identification document, on the one hand, and a passport, UMID, or driver’s license, on the other (Philippine Statistics Authority, n.d.). The NBI row follows that agency’s statement that two government-issued identification cards are required (National Bureau of Investigation, n.d.). The professional-license row is deliberately incomplete: the items are whatever the Professional Regulation Commission charter states for the profession, and they are not invented here.

[88|Normal] Modeling
[89|Normal] Modeling runs Apriori, FP-Growth, and Eclat on the primary matrix. Apriori builds candidates level by level and discards a candidate that contains an infrequent subset (Agrawal & Srikant, 1994). FP-Growth compresses the transactions into a frequent-pattern tree and reads the frequent itemsets from the tree (Han et al., 2000). Eclat intersects transaction-identifier lists (Zaki, 2000). All three are called from SPMF so that the comparison is not a comparison of unrelated code bases (Fournier-Viger et al., 2016). Python prepares the matrix and reads the SPMF output. The same input file, the same minimum support, and the same minimum confidence are passed to each algorithm.
[90|Normal] Minimum support is chosen after the item-frequency distribution is inspected, at a value that still allows itemsets of size two and size three to appear if the matrix contains them. Minimum confidence is chosen on the same inspection and is applied when rules are generated from the frequent itemsets. Both values will be stated with the results. They are not fixed in this method section, because fixing them before the distribution is known would hide the sparsity of the matrix. Lift is not an input to the miners. It is computed for every generated rule as confidence divided by the support of the consequent (Han et al., 2012). The sensitivity matrix is mined with the same settings and is reported in a separate table.
[91|Normal] Evaluation
[92|Normal] Evaluation answers the third research question with measures that are defined before the run. Let N be the number of services in the matrix. For an itemset S, support is the number of services whose baskets contain S, divided by N. For a rule X → Y, confidence is the support of X ∪ Y divided by the support of X. Lift is that confidence divided by the support of Y. A lift above 1 means the consequent occurs with the antecedent more often than the consequent’s overall frequency would suggest. A lift of 1 is independence. A lift below 1 is a negative association. Rules with lift at or below 1 remain in the evaluation table, because hiding them would overstate the structure of the matrix. They are excluded from route construction.
[93|Normal] The algorithm comparison uses four descriptions, all computed on the primary matrix. The first is the number of frequent itemsets returned by each algorithm. On a correct exhaustive implementation those sets are the same; a difference is treated as an implementation discrepancy and is investigated before any rule is deployed. The second is the wall-clock time of each algorithm, taken as the median of three runs on the same machine. The third is the Jaccard overlap of the frequent-itemset collections, which is 1 when the collections are identical. The fourth is the list of rules with lift greater than 1. Heaton (2016) showed that runtime depends on density and on transaction length. Those two dataset facts — items per transaction, and the share of itemsets that meet the minimum support — will be reported beside the times so that the times have a context. No accuracy, precision, or recall is defined, because the task is not a classification.
[94|Normal] The charter check is a reading, not a second miner. For each rule that is a candidate for a route, the consequent service’s current page is opened at the stored source address. The rule is confirmed when the page still requires the consequent documents whenever the antecedent documents are required. The rule is rejected when the page presents the antecedent documents as alternatives, when the page no longer lists them, or when the page was not retrieved. The confirmer records the sentence and the retrieval date. A rejected rule returns to data preparation if the cause is the encoding of alternatives, and to business understanding if the cause is the definition of a requirement. It does not return as a route.
[95|Normal] Deployment
[96|Normal] Deployment builds a sequence and a route from a rule that passed the charter check. The antecedent documents are obtained before the service that requires the consequent. Each document is joined to the office that issues it, and the consequent service is joined to the office that receives the application. When the charter check finds that the antecedent documents are alternatives, the deployment draws one branch per alternative instead of a single ordered path. Forcing one order on substitutes would contradict the charter the check just confirmed.
[97|Normal] Travel between two successive offices is the road route on OpenStreetMap, reported as distance and estimated travel time, and only when both coordinates were retrieved (Haklay & Weber, 2008). An office without a coordinate stays in the document sequence, and its leg is labeled unlocated. The starting point of a route is a locality chosen by the reader of the result, so that the same document sequence can be drawn toward the offices that serve that locality. Davao City is the locality used for the local-government services. National offices are the offices named in the charter that was mined. Figure 4 shows the shape of this step with a birth certificate, a national identification record, and a passport. The numbering is an illustration of deployment. It is not an output of Apriori, FP-Growth, or Eclat, and it is not a measured route.

[100|Normal] Scope and Ethical Considerations
[101|Normal] The study covers the agencies and the local government named in the introduction, for the service pages that can be retrieved from official sources. It does not cover every Philippine government document. It does not rank agencies, recommend a change in fees, or estimate the probability that an application will be approved. Waiting time inside an office is outside the data, so it is outside the route.
[102|Normal] The pages that are collected are published for the public. No applicant file is requested or stored. Automated collection follows robots.txt and a delay between requests. A value that is not on the page is left empty, including fees and coordinates. Charter text is quoted only as far as needed to identify a requirement, and the source address and retrieval date are kept so that a rule can be checked again when the charter changes. The charter check is done by the researchers against the public page. It does not involve a survey of applicants. Participation of agency staff is not required for the method as specified.
[103|Normal] Results and Discussion
[104|Normal] The frequent itemsets, the support, confidence, and lift of each rule, the runtime and overlap of Apriori, FP-Growth, and Eclat, and the routes that pass the charter check will be reported after those procedures have been run on the prepared matrix. This manuscript does not present those values. Presenting them before the run would invent a result the method has not produced.
[105|Normal] Conclusion
[106|Normal] The study is specified as a CRISP-DM association-rule project on official Philippine service text. The contribution claimed at this stage is the design: a transaction that counts each service once, a separation of joint requirements from alternatives, a comparison of Apriori, FP-Growth, and Eclat on one matrix, and a route that is drawn only for a rule the current charter still supports. The conclusion on which rules hold, and on which offices can be connected, waits on the run described in the method.
[107|Normal] Recommendations
[108|Normal] Recommendations that depend on the mined rules will be written with the results. Two recommendations already follow from the method. The synonym list and the source address of every requirement should be released with the matrix, so that another researcher can repeat the charter check. A charter that changes after the retrieval date should start a new data-understanding pass for that service rather than an edit of a published rule.
